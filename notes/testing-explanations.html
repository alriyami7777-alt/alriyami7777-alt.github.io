RESEARCH NOTE / EXPLAINABLE AI

An explanation needs its own evaluation

2 October 2026 · Short commentary

A clear-looking visualisation is a starting point for investigation, not proof of faithfulness.

A highlighted time step or feature can make a model easier to discuss. It does not, by itself, show that the highlighted information caused the prediction. Prediction quality and explanation quality need separate evidence.

Jain and Wallace examined this problem for attention in NLP models. Adebayo and colleagues approached a related question through saliency maps, showing why visual appeal is an unreliable basis for evaluating explanations. These studies are methodological starting points; their findings should not be treated as direct tests of an insider-threat detector.

For behavioural security models, useful questions include whether explanations respond when the learned model changes, whether removing supposedly important information affects the prediction, and whether the result survives reasonable changes to the reference or perturbation procedure.

Those checks also have limits: a perturbation can create unrealistic activity, and correlated features can complicate attribution. Reporting the explanation method, test design, and limitations makes a visualisation much more informative than presenting it alone.
