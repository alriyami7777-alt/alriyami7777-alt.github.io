RESEARCH NOTE / DATASETS

Know what your dataset represents

2 October 2026 · Short commentary

Why the origin of a security event matters as much as its label.

The label “insider-threat dataset” says little about how its records were produced. Activity may come from an exercise, a simulator, or an existing synthetic benchmark. These sources support different conclusions about realism and generalisation.

The SPEDIA dataset study provides a useful example: its methodology combines observations from a controlled cyber exercise with simulated behaviour and CERT-derived synthetic data. That combination is valuable, but it also makes the origin of each subset important when designing a new experiment.

For a transfer study, a practical question follows: is the target independent of the training source? Shared source material or preprocessing assumptions can blur the intended boundary. Recording which subsets were used, how labels were attached, and which transformations were learned from training data makes the experiment easier to inspect.

A dataset description should therefore accompany the results, rather than function as background decoration. Provenance helps readers understand what the evidence supports—and what still needs testing elsewhere.
